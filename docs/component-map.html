Aangan Studio, Phone Enquiry Agent: Components Map

Case 03, incoming calls only. A call is a conversation, so the map has a loop: Vaani carries the call and sends every caller turn to our server. On each turn Gemini decides using qualified.md, a rules check guards it, and Gemini then speaks using the Aangan skill. The call only ends on a final route. Scheduling only starts after Gemini decides the lead is qualified; then the auto-scheduler books the consultation in Cal.com from the transcript. Neon holds the record and HubSpot shows it. WhatsApp and the web form can reuse the same context, decision and output layers.

One call, step by step

  1. The caller dials. Vaani answers on the first ring, so the five-minute promise is met in seconds, day or night, and opens a WebSocket to our server for the call.
  2. After each thing the caller says, Vaani sends the conversation so far. Our server looks up the caller's history in Neon.
  3. Gemini decides with qualified.md only: it scores the five criteria with evidence and picks a route.
  4. The rules check can overrule Gemini: a location outside the service-area list, a lead time under the minimum, or an out-of-scope project type.
  5. Gemini then writes the spoken reply from the Aangan skill for that route, and the price filter checks it before Vaani speaks it. Clarify asks one question and loops. Qualify asks when the caller is free, then says a time will be confirmed right after. Decline closes politely. Escalate promises a senior call-back.
  6. When the call ends, Vaani's call_postprocessing webhook delivers the transcript. Neon stores everything first. Email (Resend) and HubSpot are fed from that record, so nothing is lost if an email or a sync fails.
  7. Scheduling is gated on the decision. Only when Gemini's final route is Qualify, and the rules check agrees, does the saved record start the auto-scheduler. Decline, Clarify and Escalate calls are never scheduled.
  8. For a qualified call, Gemini then reads what the caller said about availability and picks the best open slot. The backend books it in Cal.com and saves the booking ID in Neon. Vaani calls the customer back to confirm, and Gemini reads that transcript. The handoff email and HubSpot deal show the booked time.

Why Gemini plus a rules check

  • Callers talk in free form. Gemini reads intent and evidence, such as "my husband knows we're calling", which a form cannot.
  • Gemini reads only qualified.md, so the decision follows Nikhil's own rubric and not general advice.
  • Hard limits are code, not judgement: the service-area list, the lead-time minimum and the no-price rule.
  • Gemini outputs structured JSON, so every decision is stored and can be audited in HubSpot.
  • A Flash-class model keeps each turn fast enough for a live call.

What Neon holds

  • calls: time, answer time, duration, outcome, transcript.
  • criteria_results: status and evidence for each of the five criteria.
  • handoffs: note, designer, claimed time, Won or Lost.
  • sync_log: what was last pushed to HubSpot, and any failures to retry.
  • bookings: Cal.com booking ID, slot, designer, status and confirmation-call attempts.
  • costs: telephony minutes, speech and Gemini tokens per call, so HubSpot can show cost per call and per booking.

Why Cal.com for scheduling

  • It already knows each designer's availability and syncs with their own calendar, so the system never guesses at free time.
  • Booking through its API blocks the slot at once, so two callers cannot get the same time.
  • The customer gets an invite and a reschedule link without extra work.
  • Webhooks report a reschedule or cancel back to Neon, so HubSpot and the emails stay correct.

Why HubSpot sits on top of Neon

  • Neon is the system of record: every call, decision and cost, written first.
  • HubSpot is where Nikhil and the designers already work with leads. Each qualified call becomes a contact and a deal.
  • Deal stages (Enquiry, Qualified, Consultation booked, Won, Lost) give conversion by response time without a manual pull.
  • Call cost and answer time go in as custom properties, so the tool's own cost shows on the same dashboard.

The Cut

Nikhil asked for standard per-square-foot quotes on the call. The map does not build it.

  • pricing.md says indicative only and varies by site, materials and timeline.
  • His own rubric says never quote and never probe on budget.
  • In T02 and T13 the callers accepted the deflection and still booked.
  • So pricing.md is given to neither Gemini nor Vaani. Gemini says the playbook's fixed pricing line, and the price filter blocks any figure in every spoken reply.

Handoff channel: Resend email

  • Email reaches designers on phone and desktop with no extra app to join, and Gmail inboxes work.
  • The backend sends it through Resend with an idempotency key, so a retried job never sends the same email twice.
  • The note carries the caller's number and signed Claim, Won and Lost links. A link opens a confirmation page, so a mail scanner cannot change a lead.
  • Trade-off: an email can sit unread longer than a chat push. Escalations get their own subject line and go straight to Nikhil's address.

Open questions for Nikhil

  • Minimum lead time: services.md says 6 weeks, qualified.md says 8 to 10. T15 was booked at 6.
  • Commercial minimum: the skill says 500 sq ft (from T18), but services.md and qualified.md do not state it.
  • Fringe areas such as Talegaon: qualified.md says decline, the skill says hold for the studio head. The map follows qualified.md for the decision. Confirm which rule wins.
  • Who is the named senior person for escalations, and what is the after-hours rule?
  • Which designers' calendars connect to Cal.com, and do site visits and phone consultations need separate event types?
  • The map assumes the confirmation is an outbound Vaani call, with an email task after two missed tries. Should it be WhatsApp or SMS instead?
  • Latency: each turn runs two Gemini calls in a row, about 3 seconds, against Vaani's advertised 450 ms. Tune after a real call, for example by merging the two calls.
  • Does the current HubSpot plan allow custom properties and the dashboards needed at about 200 enquiries a month?
